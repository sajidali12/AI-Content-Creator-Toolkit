# Content Idea Scoring Framework

Use this framework to compare candidate ideas, not to predict exact performance. Score each dimension from 1 to 5, multiply by its weight, and normalize to 100.

## Weighted dimensions

| Dimension | Weight | What a high score means |
|---|---:|---|
| Audience relevance | 20 | Strong match to a known audience need, desire, question, identity, or entertainment interest |
| Creator fit and credibility | 15 | The creator has experience, access, genuine interest, or a distinctive perspective |
| Clarity of promise | 10 | The audience quickly understands what they will get |
| Differentiation | 15 | A fresh angle, evidence, access, format, story, or useful point of view |
| Timeliness / demand signal | 10 | There is credible current interest, a timely trigger, or recurring demand |
| Story / demonstration potential | 10 | The idea can be shown, experienced, tested, or told compellingly |
| Feasibility | 10 | Realistic within available time, budget, skills, access, and risk |
| Strategic value | 10 | Supports the creator's stated goals: community, authority, reach, leads, sales, learning, or expression |

Formula: `weighted score = sum((dimension score / 5) * dimension weight)`

## Score interpretation

- **85–100: Strong candidate.** Worth prioritizing if claims are verifiable and production is feasible.
- **70–84: Promising.** Improve the angle, evidence, hook, or feasibility before production.
- **55–69: Needs refinement.** Could work for a specific sub-audience or with a stronger story.
- **Below 55: Deprioritize.** Weak fit, unclear value, generic angle, or poor feasibility.

These are editorial decision bands, not performance forecasts.

## Hard gates

Regardless of numerical score, do not recommend publishing as-is if:
- the central claim cannot be substantiated
- the idea relies on deception or harmful conduct
- it invades privacy or creates unreasonable safety risk
- it is outside the creator's capabilities or access with no viable adaptation
- it copies another creator's protected expression
- the idea's trend premise is stale or falsely described as current

## Trend score is not idea score

A trend can be highly visible and still be a poor content idea. Evaluate separately:
- **Trend signal:** evidence that attention or discussion is increasing.
- **Creator opportunity:** why this creator should cover it.
- **Audience value:** why the target audience should care.
- **Execution quality:** whether the creator can deliver on the promise.

## Portfolio balance

For a batch of ideas, avoid selecting only the highest trend scores. Consider a balanced mix:
- evergreen search/problem-solving content
- timely or trend-responsive content
- personal experience and behind-the-scenes content
- community questions and responses
- deeper authority or educational content
- experiments, reviews, comparisons, or case studies

The ideal mix depends on the creator's goals and audience; do not impose fixed percentages as universal rules.

## Explain uncertainty

Use labels such as:
- Evidence: strong / moderate / limited
- Demand signal: observed / inferred / unknown
- Effort: low / medium / high
- Confidence: high / medium / low

Never invent view forecasts, search volume, trend velocity, competitor performance, or engagement benchmarks.
