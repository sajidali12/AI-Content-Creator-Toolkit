# Analytics and Content Experiments

## 1. Start with the goal

Possible goals include:
- reach and discovery
- watch time or reading depth
- meaningful engagement
- returning audience and community
- email signups or leads
- sales or bookings
- authority, trust, and portfolio quality
- learning and creative practice

Do not assume views are the only success metric.

## 2. Metric groups

### Distribution
- impressions / reach
- traffic sources
- followers or subscribers reached
- browse, search, suggested, or external discovery where available

### Attention and retention
- average view duration
- average percentage viewed
- retention curve and drop-off points
- completion rate, where provided
- click-through rate for packaging, where provided

### Engagement
- comments and their substance
- shares
- saves
- likes or reactions
- meaningful replies and questions

### Audience development
- follows or subscriptions attributed to content, where available
- returning viewers
- repeat visits
- community participation

### Outcomes
- clicks, leads, bookings, purchases, signups, or other defined conversions
- conversion quality, not just volume

Definitions vary by platform. Use the platform's own metric definitions and reporting window.

## 3. Fair comparison

Compare content with similar:
- platform and format
- duration or length
- audience and language
- publication age and measurement window
- distribution conditions
- campaign or paid promotion status

Do not compare a 24-hour result with a 90-day result without explaining the difference.

## 4. Diagnose patterns carefully

Possible interpretations (not guaranteed causes):
- Strong reach, weak retention: topic/packaging may attract the wrong expectation, or opening/pacing may need work.
- Strong retention, low reach: the content may satisfy a narrow audience; distribution and topic packaging may be worth testing.
- Strong saves: content may be useful as a reference.
- Strong shares: content may be relatable, surprising, or useful to others.
- Many views, few follows: the video may be entertaining but not communicate the creator's ongoing value.
- Good clicks, weak conversion: landing page, offer, audience match, or friction may be involved.

Always consider alternative explanations and sample size.

## 5. Experiment design

1. State the question.
2. Identify one main variable to test: topic angle, opening, duration, visual format, title/cover, or CTA.
3. Define the audience and comparable posts.
4. Set a measurement window.
5. Define success before running the test.
6. Keep other factors as stable as practical.
7. Review results and qualitative feedback.
8. Record what was learned and the next experiment.

Do not overstate causal conclusions from small, uncontrolled samples.

## 6. Simple content review

For each post, capture:
- date and platform
- topic and content pillar
- format and duration
- hook / title / cover
- goal
- metrics at a consistent interval
- audience comments and questions
- what seemed to work
- what did not work
- one next experiment

Use `templates/CONTENT-CALENDAR.md` for a lightweight planning and review log.

## 7. Privacy

Remove names, handles, emails, phone numbers, order details, and other identifying information before sharing private audience messages or analytics exports. Do not expose private customer or follower data in public examples.
