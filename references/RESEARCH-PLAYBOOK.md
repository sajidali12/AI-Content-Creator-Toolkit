# Research Playbook: Find Real Content Opportunities

## 1. Research objectives

Research should answer:
1. What is happening or what does the audience need?
2. Who cares, and in which geography or community?
3. What evidence supports the opportunity?
4. Why is this creator a good person to cover it?
5. What fresh angle can they add?
6. What should be checked before publishing?

## 2. Opportunity sources

### Audience-owned signals
- Comments, DMs, FAQs, polls, community posts
- Repeated questions and objections
- Customer or student questions (with private information removed)
- Support tickets, reviews, and product feedback
- Questions asked during live sessions, classes, or events

### Search and discovery signals
- Search suggestions and related questions
- Search trends and keyword tools, when available
- Platform search results and topic pages
- Public forums and niche communities
- Newsletters, specialist publications, and industry reports

### Timely signals
- Official product launches and release notes
- Events, festivals, seasons, conferences, and local developments
- Policy or industry changes
- New research and public datasets
- Product changes, pricing, availability, and feature rollouts

### Creator-specific signals
- Personal experiments and lessons
- Local knowledge, access, interviews, and demonstrations
- Behind-the-scenes processes
- Mistakes, myths, comparisons, and case studies
- An unanswered question in the creator's own content library

## 3. Source quality hierarchy

Prefer, in order:
1. Primary sources: original research, official announcements, documentation, public records, direct interviews, first-party data.
2. Reputable specialist or mainstream reporting with named sources and dates.
3. Established community discussions as qualitative signals, not proof of factual claims.
4. Aggregators, social posts, and search snippets only as leads to verify.

A primary source can still be promotional or incomplete. Explain its limitations.

## 4. Trend verification

For each time-sensitive claim, record:
- source and link
- publication date
- event or launch date, if different
- geography and platform relevance
- status: announced, testing, beta, waitlist, rolling out, generally available, discontinued, or unknown
- what is confirmed vs inferred
- last checked date when useful

Do not confuse:
- a viral post with a broad trend
- a single anecdote with representative demand
- a product announcement with public availability
- a launch date with a publication date
- a rising keyword with proven purchase intent
- high search interest with a good fit for this creator

When tools support it, triangulate with multiple independent sources. For important claims, link directly to the underlying source rather than only to a commentary article.

## 5. Research workflow

1. Define niche, target audience, market, platform, and time window.
2. Generate a set of research queries across audience questions, current events, search interest, communities, and official sources.
3. Gather candidate signals.
4. Open and inspect the strongest sources.
5. Verify dates, availability, and claims.
6. Look for counter-evidence and competing interpretations.
7. Identify content gaps: what is missing, unclear, inaccessible, too technical, or not localized?
8. Create distinct angles for this creator.
9. Score the resulting ideas using `IDEA-SCORING.md`.
10. Present sources and limitations clearly.

## 6. Search query patterns

Adapt these patterns to the niche and market:
- `[audience] common questions about [topic]`
- `site:[relevant community domain] [specific problem]`
- `[industry] latest official update [month year]`
- `[product or organization] release notes [feature]`
- `[topic] beginner mistakes`
- `[topic] myths vs evidence`
- `[location] [activity] seasonal guide`
- `[audience] alternatives to [popular solution]`
- `[topic] case study results methodology`
- `[topic] unanswered questions`

Do not assume every query has a reliable search-volume result.

## 8. When browsing is unavailable

Say that live research is unavailable in this session. Offer evergreen ideas, analyze user-provided sources, or create a research checklist. Never label an idea "trending now" without current evidence.
